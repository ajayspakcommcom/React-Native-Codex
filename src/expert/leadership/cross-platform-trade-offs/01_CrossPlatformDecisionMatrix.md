# Cross-Platform Decision Matrix

## Objective
- Choose a platform strategy based on product constraints, team shape, and operational cost.
- Avoid ideology-driven decisions.

## React Native is strongest when
- the team has strong JavaScript and React depth
- product velocity matters across iOS and Android
- shared business logic and shared UI matter
- native capabilities are needed, but not dominant everywhere

## Flutter is strongest when
- the organization wants a single UI toolkit with high rendering consistency
- the team is comfortable investing in Flutter-specific expertise
- product leadership accepts a more framework-centric ecosystem bet

## Fully native is strongest when
- platform-specific UX is a strategic differentiator
- deep OS integration is constant, not occasional
- performance and platform-first experience outweigh shared-code efficiency
- iOS and Android teams are staffed strongly enough to sustain separate delivery

## Enterprise decision drivers
- hiring market and current team strength
- release velocity expectations
- native integration depth
- design-system portability
- long-term maintenance cost
- observability and release operations maturity
- performance sensitivity of the product

## Decision matrix
### Choose React Native when
- cross-platform delivery speed is a priority
- shared team structure is valuable
- native work is important but not the majority of the roadmap

### Choose Flutter when
- UI consistency and a single rendering model are valued above ecosystem familiarity
- the company is willing to standardize around Flutter expertise

### Choose fully native when
- native platform differentiation is central to the product
- advanced OS capabilities dominate the roadmap
- dedicated iOS and Android organizations already exist

## Leadership rule
- choose the platform that fits the organization you actually have, not the one you wish you had
